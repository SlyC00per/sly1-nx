# The iPhone version

Work in progress, on the `ios` branch (started 2026-11-26). The aim is the same game on an
iPhone, from the same tree: the game code, sound, video, saves and the disc reader are shared
with the Switch version, and what is new is a renderer for Apple's GPUs and a platform layer
for iOS.

## Plan

1. **A Metal renderer** (`port/apple/gpu_metal.mm`), written and checked on the Mac, whose GPU
   is of the same family as the iPhone's. In progress: see below.
2. **A window on the Mac**, so the Metal picture can be seen and played there. Done: see
   "The Mac's window".
3. **The iOS layer** (`port/ios/`): the app, the disc image found in the app's folder, saves,
   a controller through Apple's GameController framework, suspension when the app leaves the
   screen.
4. **The Xcode project and the first run on a phone.** The project is generated by CMake and
   builds; the first run is to come. See "The app".

Done on the Mac, for both systems (2026-11-26): sound through an audio queue
(`port/apple/audio_out.cpp`), a controller through the GameController framework
(`port/apple/pad_gc.mm`), and a picture of the screen's own shape (below).

## The app

`port/ios/app.mm` is the app: a UIKit application with one scene, whose view's layer is the
screen the Metal renderer presents to. The game is the Mac's program's own entry
(`port/host/main.cpp`, `sly1_main` in this build) on a thread of its own.

- **The disc image** is looked for in the app's Documents folder, by its contents, every two
  seconds until one is there; the screen says so meanwhile. The folder shows in the Files app
  (On My iPhone) and in the Finder with the phone connected. Saves are in `save/` beside it.
  Until one is there the screen also has a button that opens the system's file browser (iCloud
  Drive, On My iPhone, a drive): the chosen file is copied into the Documents folder, so it is
  asked for once. A file that is not a disc image to play is not kept, and the reason is shown.
- **The pad** is a controller when one is connected. Without one the screen is: a touch that
  begins on the left is the left stick, one on the right the camera, and the buttons are drawn
  over the picture (hidden while a controller is connected).
- **Off the screen** (another app, the lock) nothing may be given to the GPU: the frame's end
  waits (`port::gpu::Suspend`), the game with it, and the sound is paused.
- **The picture** has its view's shape and up to 1,080 lines, and follows the view: the phone
  opened or closed while the game runs changes the game's view and the frame's images at the
  next frame (`port::gpu::SetScreenShape`).
- **Folded half way** (`UIHingeInteraction`, iOS 27.1: the hinge partly open and under about
  150 degrees) the app turns upright, which puts the hinge across the view's middle: the
  picture is the upper half and the controls are on the lower half. (Kept on its side, the
  system shows the app small across the hinge.) The hinge's updates are in the log.
- **The icon** is `SLY1_IOS_ICON`, a square picture of your own, or `branding/local/ios-icon.jpg`
  (not committed), or else the project's mark.
- What the program prints goes to `sly1-log.txt` in the Documents folder (the run before's to
  `sly1-log-before.txt`).

Building it (Xcode, with its iOS SDK):

```sh
cmake -B build-ios -G Xcode -DCMAKE_SYSTEM_NAME=iOS -DPLATFORM=HOST \
    -DSLY1_ISO="/path/to/your/Sly Cooper.iso" \
    -DSLY1_IOS_TEAM=YOURTEAMID -DSLY1_IOS_BUNDLE_ID=an.identifier.of.your.own
xcodebuild -project build-ios/sly1_nx.xcodeproj -target sly1_ios -configuration Release \
    -sdk iphoneos -allowProvisioningUpdates
```

or open `build-ios/sly1_nx.xcodeproj`, choose the `sly1_ios` target and a phone, and run. With
a free Apple ID the app opens for seven days after each install.

State (2026-11-26): it runs on an iPhone Duo (A20 Pro, iOS 27.2.1): picture, sound, the
screen's controls, leaving and coming back. Not checked on the phone yet: the picture's size
following the screen, the half-folded layout and the file browser (written after that run;
the half screen's shape, 2670:939, was drawn on the Mac). The game steps by a sixtieth of a
second at least, so 120 frames a second is a change to the game's clock, not to the app.

## The picture's shape

The phone's screens are not 16:9 (the iPhone Duo's are about 1.42:1 inside and 1.45:1 outside).
The port's wide view is not fixed at 16:9: `port::WideFactor()` is the picture's width over the
PS2's 4:3 one, and everything that widens the view or places the 2D elements goes by it (the
camera, the culling, `Wide2D`, the movies' zoom). `SLY1_ASPECT=W:H` sets it before the game
starts, and `port::SetAspect` at any time; the view is never narrower than the PS2's. The Metal
renderer's images are 720 lines of the width that shape gives on the Mac.

`SLY1_ASPECT=2670:1878` on the Mac gives a window of the Duo's inner screen's shape: the level's
picture fills it with nothing stretched, and a movie keeps its own black bars above and below.
On the phone the shape is the picture's view's, whenever it changes.

## The Metal renderer

It takes what the GS is given to draw through the same interface as the Switch's renderer
(`port/include/port/gs_backend.h`): runs of transformed primitives with the registers they are
drawn by. The frame's two buffers and the small buffers the game draws into are images, as on
the Switch.

The pixel pipeline is not the Switch's. Apple's GPUs give a pixel shader the colour already in
the frame under its pixel, so everything the GS does after its rasteriser is done in the shader,
in the GS's own integer arithmetic, following the reference (`port/render/gs.cpp`'s `Pixel`)
line for line: the texture function, the fog, the alpha test and what a failing pixel still
writes, the destination alpha test, the blend, FBA and the frame's write mask. There is no
blend state and none of the approximations a fixed blend needs (the Switch's renderer has
several, each found by a wrong picture).

What it does not do yet:

- **Batches** (`DrawBatch`): the game's vertices are transformed by the translated VU1
  programs on the CPU, not by a vertex shader. Right, and slower than the Switch's path.
- **The outlines' width in a cut scene.** Lines are drawn as rectangles 4.5 pixels wide at 720
  lines (`SLY1_METAL_LINE=W`), the Switch renderer's width in play; its wider lines under a cut
  scene's letterbox are not followed.
- The Switch renderer's savings (parts kept from frame to frame, the effects at half size,
  the dynamic resolution). They may not be needed on this hardware.
- REGION_REPEAT texture wrapping (as REPEAT; not met in the levels run so far) and 16-bit
  frame buffers (not met either). Both are counted in the run's report.

## The Mac's window

```sh
SLY1_METAL=window build-mac/sly1_headless --iso DISC.iso --seconds 0
```

The game in a window of 1280 by 720 (resizable, kept 16:9), drawn by the Metal renderer, until
the window is closed. `--level N` starts in a level. The window has sound (`SLY1_NO_SOUND=1`:
none; `SLY1_SOUND_VOLUME=N`: hundredths). The frame rate has not been measured.

A controller the Mac knows (a DualSense, an Xbox or Switch Pro pad, over Bluetooth or USB) is
the pad too, its buttons by their places: the bottom face button is Cross. It is read with the
keyboard, 120 times a second. No rumble yet.

The keyboard is the pad:

| Keys | Pad |
| --- | --- |
| W A S D | left stick (move) |
| arrows | right stick (camera) |
| Space, F, E, Q | Cross (jump), Square (cane), Circle, Triangle |
| 1, 2, 3, 4 | L1 (Binocucom), R1, L2, R2 |
| Return, Tab | Start, Select |
| I J K L | the direction buttons |

## Checking it against the reference

The Mac's build has the reference drawing (`--dump-fb`: the GS's primitives rasterised into
its memory as the GS does). With a renderer attached too, both draw the same primitives, so
each frame the Metal renderer shows can be compared with the reference's picture of the same
frame, pixel by pixel:

```sh
SLY1_METAL=1 SLY1_METAL_COMPARE=1 SLY1_GS_HALF_LINE=1 SLY1_LEVEL_FRAMES=240 \
    build-mac/sly1_headless --iso DISC.iso --levels 5,20 --dump-fb DIR --dump-every 160
```

- `SLY1_METAL=1`: the Mac's build draws with Metal (without it nothing changes there).
- `SLY1_METAL_COMPARE=1`: the images at the GS's own size (640 by 224), textures filtered as
  the GS's registers say, the game's motion blur drawn (the port leaves it out; the reference
  has it), and each frame compared. `SLY1_METAL_COMPARE_EACH=N` prints every N-th frame's
  numbers; the run's report has the totals.
- `SLY1_GS_HALF_LINE=1`: the odd field's half line kept in what the renderer is given, as the
  reference has it.
- `SLY1_METAL_DUMP=DIR` (`SLY1_METAL_DUMP_EVERY=N`): the Metal frames as `metal-NNNNN.png`,
  beside the reference's `frame-NNNNN.png`.
- `SLY1_METAL_SIZE=WxH`, `SLY1_METAL_FILTER=gs|linear`, `SLY1_METAL_BLUR=0|1`: the images'
  size, the textures' filter and the blur, when not comparing.

The comparison means nothing while a movie plays: a movie's pictures go to the renderer
directly and never reach the GS's memory, so the reference's picture is not the movie.

**First result (2026-11-26, Apple M2 Max, levels 5 and 20, 240 frames each).** In the levels,
the mean difference of a colour is 0.5 to 0.7 of 255; 96 to 97% of the pixels are within 2 of
the reference in every colour, 98 to 99% within 8, 99.5% within 32. What is left has not been
looked into yet (edges and lines, where the GPU's rasteriser and the reference's differ, are
the likely share).
